Power rankings

Every FBS team played against every other, on a neutral field in good weather (75°F, dry, light wind), using the same weighted advanced-stat model as the matchup pages. Rating is a team's average projected margin over all of those games: how many points better it is than an average FBS team on a neutral field. Each projection blends play-by-play analytics with results (game control: how much of each real game a team was in control of, adjusted for schedule), about half each.

iHow to read this table

Every number comes from one big simulation: each FBS team projected against every other FBS team (about 9,400 games) on a neutral field in good weather (75°F, dry, light wind), using the same model as the matchup pages. "Average team" means an average FBS team.

Rating
What it means: how many points better this team is than an average FBS team on a neutral field.
How it's estimated: the team's average projected margin across all ~137 simulated games. Each projection blends the two columns to the right, play-by-play and results, about half each (0.70 × play-by-play margin + 0.55 × results gap). When both agree a team is strong, the blend goes beyond either one: in past seasons each was too cautious about lopsided games on its own.
Play-by-play
What it means: the team's rating from the advanced stats alone, in points vs an average team, with its rank on that measure.
How it's estimated: expected points added (EPA) per play for each offense and defense, weighted toward the most relevant past games, adjusted for opponent strength (every offense, defense and conference solved together, so schedules count), blended with last season's schedule-adjusted numbers early in the year, and converted to points.
Results
What it means: the team's rating from what actually happened in its games, in points vs an average team, with its rank on that measure.
How it's estimated: game control. A win-probability model tracks each team's chance of winning snap by snap (score, time left, possession, field position; no pregame expectations) and averages it over the 60 minutes. Leading all day scores high even in a 10-point win; a game tied late that ends in a blowout counts for much less than the score suggests. Then it's adjusted for opponents and home field and put on a points scale.
Avg score vs avg team
What it means: the typical final score of this team's simulated games.
How it's estimated: average projected points scored and allowed across all the simulated games. Total points come from the play-by-play model; the split between the teams follows the blended margin.
Beats avg team
What it means: the chance this team beats an average FBS team on a neutral field.
How it's estimated: average win probability across the simulated games. Each game's chance comes from the projected margin and how much it typically misses by (about 15–16 points), fit on past seasons.
Favored in
What it means: how many of the other FBS teams this team would be favored to beat on a neutral field.
How it's estimated: a count of the simulated games where its projected margin is above zero.

How the rankings work

For each pair of FBS teams the model projects a neutral-site game in good weather, exactly as the Build a matchup page would. Each team's profile is weighted toward its past games against opponents most like the one it would face, recent weeks, similar settings and its current QB, then blended with last season's numbers when the sample is small.

That's about 9,400 projected games. Rating is the average projected margin; the score shows average points scored and allowed; "Beats avg team" is the average win probability; "Favored in" counts the projected wins.

Play-by-play and results. Every projected margin blends the play-by-play model with each team's results rating, built on game control: in every real game this season, a win-probability model tracks each team's chance of winning snap by snap (from score, time left, possession and field position, with no pregame expectations), and game control is that chance averaged over the 60 minutes. A team that led all day scores high even if it won by 10; a game that was tied late and ended in a blowout counts for much less than the final score suggests. Game control is then adjusted for schedule and home field, solving for all teams and conferences at once, and put on a points scale. The blend was fit on past seasons so the two have about equal say, and it predicted games better than play-by-play alone. The Play-by-play and Results columns show each view on its own (points vs an average team, with that view's rank) so you can see where they disagree. The blended rating is usually larger than either: when both views agree a team is strong, the evidence adds up, and in past seasons each view on its own was too cautious about lopsided games.

Strength of schedule. Every game's EPA is adjusted for the opponent, and opponent strength is solved for every offense, defense and conference at once (FBS and FCS games), so a defense that looked great against weak offenses doesn't make its opponents look better than they are. Conference strength pools all of a conference's nonconference games, and last season's starting point is schedule-adjusted the same way. In backtests on 2024–26 games this made the site's predictions more accurate.

The rankings measure how teams have played per snap, not their records or who they've beaten, so a team with close losses can rank above teams with better records.
